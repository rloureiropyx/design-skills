# Framing beats

Read this only when the user wants the frame explained to people who were not in the interview — a scrollytelling article, a deck, a walkthrough.

`FRAMED_PROBLEM.md` is written for `shaping` and for someone who will act on it. It is a working document: registers, IDs, dispositions, statuses. Nobody reads it to understand anything. When the frame needs to be understood rather than used, it needs a narrative, and narrative is `pitch`'s job — audience, goal, tone, and which beats survive. This file is a **draft beat set** to hand `pitch` so it starts from the frame rather than from a blank page. `pitch` may cut, merge, or reorder any of it.

## What to do

1. Confirm the frame first. Never build an explainer from an unconfirmed frame — it hardens a draft into something people quote.
2. Run `pitch` over `FRAMED_PROBLEM.md`, handing it the beats below as a starting draft and saying they are a draft.
3. `pitch` writes `PITCH.md`. Then `scrollytelling` turns that into the article.

Say in one line that the article is a **communication artifact**. `FRAMED_PROBLEM.md` stays the north star. When the two disagree, the document is right and the article is stale — and an article that has drifted is worse than none, because it is the version people remember.

## The draft beats

| # | Beat | Where it comes from | What it has to land |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The request, exactly as it arrived | `## Source request` | What someone asked for, in their words, including the solution baked into it |
| 2 | What people actually said | `## Source register` — two or three quotes | Real voices, attributed. This is the evidence the rest rests on |
| 3 | The episode | `## Baseline story` | One person, one moment, one consequence. Concrete enough to picture |
| 4 | Why it keeps happening | `## System context` — the iceberg | The arrangement underneath the episode, not just the episode |
| 5 | The explanation everyone assumed | `## Alternative frames considered` — the original frame | Fair, not a straw man. It was plausible, which is why it was believed |
| 6 | The explanations that also fit | Remaining alternatives | The same evidence supporting more than one story |
| 7 | What settled it | `## Evidence ledger`, `## Options landscape` | The specific observation or signal that made one frame win |
| 8 | What cannot move | `## Constraint registry` — the `C` rows | The walls, with who owns them. This is what makes the frame feel real |
| 9 | The frame | `## Chosen problem frame`, `## Working question` | One sentence someone can repeat accurately afterwards |
| 10 | What we chose not to know | `## Open questions` — accepted risks | The risks taken on purpose, with names. Rare in a walkthrough and the reason people trust it |
| 11 | What happens next | `## Handoff` | Time to explore, not a commitment to build. Say it plainly |

Eleven beats is more than most articles want. Beats 5–7 are the argument and usually survive; 2, 8, and 10 are what make it credible rather than a story; 4 is the one to cut first if the iceberg turned out thin. `pitch` decides.

## What not to do here

- **Do not write new claims.** Every beat compresses something already in the frame. If a beat needs a fact the frame does not have, the frame is incomplete — go back rather than inventing a transition.
- **Do not soften the accepted risks.** Beat 10 is the one people are tempted to drop, and it is the one that makes the rest believable.
- **Do not let the article become the document.** If someone starts asking for the frame to change so the story flows better, that is the frame changing for the wrong reason.
