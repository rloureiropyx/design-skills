# Goal and tone

Read this before the interview, in step 1.

The two questions that change everything downstream are *what is this for* and *what does it sound like*. Asked open, they get answered with "informative, engaging" and the draft ends up generic. Asked as a menu, they get answered in five seconds and the draft has a spine.

Present both menus in the same message as the other two questions. Recommend one of each, based on whatever the user has already told you, and say why in half a line. They pick, adjust, or write their own.

## Goal — what the piece is for

Pick one. Two goals means two pieces.

| # | Goal | The reader ends up… | Shape it implies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | **Explain how something works** | able to describe the mechanism | Build up one system, part by part. Diagram-led. 6–10 beats. |
| G2 | **Argue a position** | persuaded, or clear about the disagreement | State the claim early, spend the middle on evidence, close on the consequence. 5–8 beats. |
| G3 | **Tell what happened** | knowing the story and its outcome | Chronological. Tension in beat 1, turn in the middle, result at the end. 6–12 beats. |
| G4 | **Teach a technique** | able to do it themselves | One step per beat, worked example throughout. Ends with the reader's first move. 7–12 beats. |
| G5 | **Walk through a case** | able to judge whether it applies to them | Situation, decision, result. Real specifics, including what did not work. 6–10 beats. |
| G6 | **Orient a newcomer** | knowing the landscape and where to start | Broad to narrow. Names things, does not go deep. 5–8 beats. |
| G7 | **Show the work** | seeing the craft and the decisions in it | The artifact is what gets shown; beats are the decisions behind it. 5–9 beats. |

The beat counts are starting points, not budgets. A strong 6 beats a padded 10 in every row.

They are also medium-independent. One beat is not one scene and not one slide — a renderer may split a beat across a build or merge two that share a visual state. Do not adjust the count for the deliverable; adjust the *budget* recorded in step 1 instead.

## Tone — what it sounds like

Pick one. This decides sentence length, how much the piece hedges, and whether it is allowed to be funny.

| # | Tone | Sounds like | Rules it sets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | **Plain and direct** | a good colleague explaining something at a whiteboard | Short sentences. No metaphor unless it does real work. Says "this is hard" when it is. |
| T2 | **Reflective** | someone thinking out loud, in public | First person allowed. Doubt allowed. Ends less certain than it started, on purpose. |
| T3 | **Technical and precise** | documentation with a narrative | Exact terms, no approximations, numbers where numbers exist. Never dramatises. |
| T4 | **Opinionated** | an editorial with a name on it | Takes a side in beat 1. Names what it disagrees with. Still fair to the other side. |
| T5 | **Warm and conversational** | a talk, not a paper | Contractions, asides, direct address. Still says something. |
| T6 | **Dry and understated** | the joke is in the restraint | Deadpan. Lets the absurdity of the situation carry itself. Hardest to sustain — only pick it if the material is genuinely absurd. |

## What the choice actually changes

Record both at the top of `PITCH.md`, and hold them while drafting. Concretely:

- **The opening beat.** G2 opens on a claim; G3 opens on a moment; G6 opens on a map. Getting beat 1 wrong is the most common reason a draft feels off, and it is almost always a goal mismatch rather than a writing problem.
- **What the SHOW field asks for.** G1 and G4 need visuals that build. G3 and G5 need visuals that show change over time. G7's visual *is* the subject. G2 usually needs the fewest and the strongest.
- **How much hedging is allowed.** T3 hedges precisely and often; T4 barely hedges at all; T2 hedges as the point. Mixing them reads as evasiveness.
- **Sentence length in the claims.** T1 and T6 run short. T2 and T5 can run long. T3 runs medium and dense.

## The tone and the voice profile are not the same question

The tone decides the register — how much this piece hedges, how long its sentences run, whether it is allowed to be funny. `WRITING_STYLE.md` decides what that register sounds like in this person's hands. `T1 Plain and direct` is a category; the profile is why this writer's plain and direct does not read like anyone else's.

When a profile rule contradicts the chosen tone, name the conflict and pick one, in half a line. Do not average them.

## When the user will not pick

Some people answer "a bit of all of them". Do not average — averaging produces the generic draft the menu exists to avoid.

Recommend one, draft it, and say: *"I wrote this as G2/T4. If it reads too combative, T1 is the same argument with the edges off — say the word and I'll rewrite the claims, not the structure."* Tone is cheap to change after the beats are agreed. Goal is not.

## Combinations that do not work

- **G1 with T4** — explaining how something works while arguing about it means the reader cannot tell which parts are mechanism and which are opinion.
- **G4 with T2** — a reader following instructions needs the author to be sure. Reflective doubt in a tutorial reads as "this might not work".
- **G6 with T3** — a newcomer does not have the vocabulary that precision requires. Orientation needs plainness.

If the user picks one of these, say so and offer the nearest pair that works. Then do what they say.
