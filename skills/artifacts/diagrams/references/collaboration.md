# Diagramming With Other People

From *Stuck? Diagrams Help*, Part 5. Load this when a diagram is being made *with* a person or group, or to move a group toward a decision. Core belief: **a diagram is "an object of discourse — it only matters as much as the discourse it spurs."** Judge it by the conversation and decisions it produces, not its polish. And: **include people in the process — "the results are always better,"** buy-in and trust included.

## Eight transferable moves (each from a case study)

- **Change the center.** When a standard structure reinforces the wrong thing (e.g. an org chart centering management), re-center on *process, outcome, or the user*. "Try a new format or out-of-the-box way of visualizing something old, tired, or triggering, to get people to look at the content with new eyes."
- **Agree on definitions.** When people talk past each other, the culprit is usually mismatched vocabulary. Diagram the *definitions and how concepts relate* to force a shared model. **"When words get hard, turn to pictures."**
- **Address collective anxiety.** When an unspoken fear keeps derailing the discussion, *add a layer to the existing diagram that answers it* (e.g. ownership + effort icons to answer "who does all this work?"). Read the room; "call an audible."
- **Expand the assignment.** When a diagram surfaces a problem bigger than your task, use the diagram itself to convene the right people. "See something, say something." Introduce it "as a territory to explore on a well-defined quest."
- **Struck by a vision.** Good forms often emerge from *testing content in another form* and from *stepping away*. Always ask potential users for feedback; "allow space to wander — if it feels hard, do something else."
- **Provoke conversation.** To deliver a hard, predetermined conclusion, **start with a blank but trusted framework and let the group fill it in** so they reach the conclusion themselves. When reality doesn't fit, **break the framework together** (builds trust that it's genuine discovery). Then know when to be silent.
- **Ask obvious questions.** A beginner + an expert co-editing produces clarity neither reaches alone. Make a **"straw-diagram to burn for warmth"** (a deliberately messy draft as bait for the expert to improve), then **work on labels together** ("can we get everything under three words per box?").
- **Change the metaphor.** When real data gets a tired, "known" response, recast it in a **visual metaphor** to change the message (content channels as planets sized by users served). "Present known data in an unknown way." "Don't hide your ideas until they're polished" — but remember the ratio: **months of observing, defining, and gathering data; five minutes of drawing. The prep is the work.**

## Facilitation checklist, by phase

**Exploring**
- Talk to people **one-on-one before** the group ("in groups we hear the edited version of the truth").
- Recruit people who *want* to work on it; assign pieces to specific people/small groups to build cross-functional trust.
- Consider opening a group session with a **blank diagram**.
- Be ready to **call an audible** — change your plan when the room requires it.

**Modeling**
- When words aren't enough, turn to pictures.
- Make a **straw-diagram to burn for warmth**; don't hide ideas until polished.
- **Break the diagram together** when reality doesn't fit the format.
- Allow space to wander; walk away when stuck.

**Delivering**
- **Read the room** instead of clinging to the plan.
- Use the diagram to shorthand a complex landscape without losing people.
- **Always ask for feedback**, especially from potential users.
- **Work on language together** — co-edit labels for real-world clarity.
- Present known data in an unknown way.

## Principles underneath
- **Model the audience's emotional state**, not just their knowledge — their feelings and context shape whether they can use the diagram as intended.
- **Know when to be silent** — provide the object of discourse, then get out of the way; let the team take it from there.
- **Bring people to a conclusion together** rather than "smacking them across the face with it." Slower is often kinder and more effective for hard messages.
- Closing ethos: *"What if we diagrammed together more and struggled apart less?"* Use diagrams to be clearer, kinder, and braver.
