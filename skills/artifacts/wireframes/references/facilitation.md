# Review facilitation

Read this before hosting a wireframe review.

The artifact and the conversation are one communication system. Plan both. **"Presenting" isn't the right word** — you host a working session.

## Before the review

- State the purpose, audience, decision, and feedback deadline.
- Decide which details are necessary now and which stay available on demand.
- Identify major decisions, constraints, implications, risks, and unresolved questions.
- Anticipate political or disciplinary perspectives without hiding disagreement.
- Rehearse the reading path, and test it with someone unfamiliar when stakes are high.
- Export a stable version and preserve an editable original.
- Open with **two lists** — what we *will* discuss today, and what we *won't*.

## Review sequence

1. **Establish context.** Reconnect the wireframe to the product objective, the scenario, the evidence, and the current stage.
2. **Explain conventions.** Define only the symbols, layers, statuses, and colour meanings needed to read it.
3. **Walk the core story.** Follow one intended path; do not begin with exceptions.
4. **Highlight decisions.** Show what changed, why, and which alternatives were considered.
5. **Name constraints and implications.** Clarify what is fixed, assumed, negotiable, or risky.
6. **Invite targeted feedback.** Ask questions that match the decision and the reviewers' expertise.
7. **Close the loop.** Capture decisions, owners, open questions, deadline, and the next version.

Per region, use **APU** — **A**ppearance → **P**urpose → **U**se. Describe what is there, say what it is for, then say how someone uses it. It stops the walkthrough from becoming a tour of boxes.

## Feedback questions

Use operational questions. Avoid "What do you think?" whenever a more specific question is possible.

- **Correctness:** what is wrong or missing in this screen?
- **Coverage:** which realistic scenario or state is absent?
- **Priority:** what must be understood first, and does the layout make that clear?
- **Feasibility:** which control, rule, dependency, or handoff creates risk?
- **Evidence:** which assertion lacks support or overstates confidence?
- **Decision:** which alternative should we choose, and under what condition?
- **Action:** what must change before this can guide implementation or research?

## The two rabbit holes

Wireframe reviews fall into the same two holes every time: **content** and **visual design**. Three legitimate responses to each — confirm it is out of scope, capture it on the won't-discuss list, or redirect to the structural question actually under review.

## Handling common failure modes

- **It becomes a copy critique** — restate whether copy is in scope and capture content issues separately.
- **It becomes visual styling** — restate the chosen fidelity and the structural decision under review.
- **Every edge case gets debated** — preserve the happy-path spine and park exceptions in a named queue.
- **Silence** — do not take it for complacency; it usually signals confusion or disagreement. Ask a specific person to test a specific scenario or assumption.
- **A new constraint appears** — record source, authority, affected decisions, and follow-up. Do not silently retrofit it.
- **The group loses the story** — zoom out, restate the objective and where you are, then continue.

## Four warning signs the requirements are unstable

These say the problem is upstream of the drawing. When they appear, stop iterating the wireframe and renegotiate objectives:

- endless reversals;
- settled decisions re-litigated;
- review sprawl with no decision;
- stakeholders talking past each other.

## After the review

Record:

```text
Artifact/version:
Decision made:
Decision owner:
Evidence or rationale:
Changes requested:
Open questions:
Next action and date:
```

Update only approved outcomes. Preserve rejected alternatives and disputed points when they explain future decisions.

Stop iterating when the artifact answers its decision.
