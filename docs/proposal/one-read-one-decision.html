A product design skill · How the proposal works

The work was done. The decision still wasn’t.

A third review ends with “let’s pick this up next week”. Not because the thinking is missing — because it is scattered across a deck, a doc, a thread, and someone’s memory. proposal assembles it into one thing a person can decide on.

Scroll to assemble the proposal

The job

Turn a chosen direction into something a person can say yes or no to.

By the time a project reaches this point, the hard thinking has happened. A problem was framed. A direction was chosen over alternatives, against numbered requirements, inside a time budget. What is usually missing is a single artifact that lets someone who was not in those conversations commit — or decline — without reopening them.

proposal assembles that artifact. It carries the frame and the direction forward rather than restating them in fresh words, and it refuses to write at all when an input it depends on is missing. Assembly, not authorship, is the whole discipline: anything that has to be invented here means a step upstream was skipped.

The output is a decision, not a document. PROPOSAL.md holds the problem, the time budget, the chosen direction, the rabbit holes, the exclusions, and what is still open — in the order a reader needs them, so one pass through it is enough to answer are we doing this?

01 · The tension

The meeting that never decides.

The questions in the room are all reasonable: how long, what is excluded, does it cover contractors, what else was considered. Every answer exists somewhere. None of them are in the thing being presented, so the meeting ends the way the last two did.

Why “proposal” and not “pitch”? Ryan Singer moved the word. A pitch is now the raw request that arrives before framing; the output of shaping is a proposal, because by then the problem, the solution ingredients, and the time to spend have been brought together. The rename does real work — “pitch” invites selling.
02 · Inputs

Assembled, not authored.

The proposal takes two documents: FRAMED_PROBLEM.md for the problem, the evidence, and the boundaries, and DIRECTIONS.md for the direction, the time budget, and the fit check that selected it. Both are carried forward, not paraphrased. Rewriting a frame in this document is how two versions of the same problem start circulating.

03 · The gate

Four reasons to refuse to write.

No framed problem, no selected direction, no time budget, or an unresolved flag on the chosen direction — any one of these stops the skill. The refusal is the useful part. A proposal written over a missing input does not solve the gap; it transfers it to whoever approves the work.

04 · Structure

Time budget near the top.

A reader who meets the solution before its cost has already formed an opinion by the time the number arrives. Put the time budget early and every paragraph after it is read as the only question that matters: is this worth six weeks? Time budget is not a schedule — it is the size of the commitment.

05 · The solution

Show the mechanism. Withhold the pixels.

The proposal presents the direction as its parts, plus one honest sentence about what it gives up. Layout, copy, states, and components are deliberately absent — pre-empting Design here is how a proposal becomes a specification nobody agreed to.

06 · The traps

Name the trap and its patch.

A rabbit hole is a specific place this work could disappear into, paired with the specific decision that prevents it. “Retro-fitting the sweep to legacy pay groups” is one; the patch is “current-generation groups only, this cycle”. “Scope creep” and “technical complexity” are not rabbit holes — they are worries. If it has no patch, it is a risk.

This is the section a builder reads twice. It is also the one most often skipped, because writing it means admitting where the plan is fragile.
07 · The exclusions

Say it here or defend it in week four.

Every exclusion was somebody’s real request. Naming them — bulk-resolve, mobile-native, changing blocker ownership — closes them for this cycle without closing them forever. Why not now, never why not ever: an exclusion written as a permanent verdict turns the proposal into a judgement on other people’s ideas.

08 · The honesty

What is still open.

Risks carried from the frame, assumptions not yet verified, the spike that has not run, and what is deliberately left for Design. A reader who discovers later something that was known now will discount every future proposal from the same team. The uncomfortable item is the one worth writing.

09 · The payoff

One read. Four possible answers.

Approve. Approve with a smaller time budget. Send it back to close a stated open item. Decline. All four are successful outcomes of a good proposal — a document that can only be approved was written to be sold. And because the reasoning stays attached, the same argument does not have to be had again next quarter.

The decision after the proposal

Four answers, all of them useful.

A proposal is written to be decided on, not to be won. Each outcome sends the work somewhere specific, and the reasoning stays with the document.

Approved

Build it. The time budget is committed and the exclusions hold. Design starts on what the proposal deliberately left open.

Approved with a smaller time budget

Back to shaping, briefly. It is worth doing at a lower price. Cut scope against the requirement statuses — never against the deadline.

Sent back

Close the open item first. Usually the spike. Run it, update DIRECTIONS.md, and re-assemble; the rest of the proposal still stands.

Declined

Record why. The frame, the direction, and the reasoning stay in the project. A declined proposal is the cheapest possible version of finding this out.

Reframe

The problem was the disagreement. When the objection is to the problem rather than the direction, the proposal is not the artifact to fix — return to problem-framing.

The payoff

Now Design can start.

A proposal ends an argument so that a different one can begin. Once the decision is made, the questions change: what does the row look like, what happens when the sweep finds nothing, how does this announce itself to a screen reader, what does the empty state say. Those are better questions, and they are only worth asking once the direction and its price are settled. The proposal is what makes them safe to ask.
