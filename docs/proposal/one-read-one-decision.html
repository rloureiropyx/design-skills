The skill

A proposal is assembly, not authoring.

A proposal is the document a group can act on without another meeting, and the last artifact written before Design starts.

proposal is one of the eight skills in this library. It reads FRAMED_PROBLEM.md and DIRECTIONS.md, compresses what they settled, and writes PROPOSAL.md. Nothing in it is new.

Every sentence traces back to the frame or to the directions document. A sentence that traces to neither is the writer starting to sell.

01 · The claim

A proposal exists to produce a decision.

A proposal exists to produce a decision. Someone reads it once and says go ahead, go ahead with less, send it back, or no. Every sentence in it traces back to the frame or to the directions document. A sentence that traces to neither is the writer starting to sell.

02 · Carried, not rewritten

Two inputs, compressed and carried.

The proposal reads two documents. The frame supplies the problem, the evidence, and the boundaries; the directions document supplies the chosen direction and the time budget. Both are compressed and carried forward. Rewriting the problem in fresh words changes it, and the version people approve stops matching the one that was evidenced.

Why "proposal" and not "pitch". Shape Up called this a pitch in 2019; Ryan Singer has since moved the word so that "pitch" names the raw request that arrives before framing. "Pitch" invites persuasion and a buyer. "Proposal" is the word people already use for a document that asks for a decision, and it names the reader's job rather than the writer's.
03 · The gate

Four things stop the writing.

Four things stop the proposal before a word is written. No framed problem, no selected direction, no time budget, or a warning flag still open on the chosen direction. Each gap means the reader would be asked to decide something no one has settled. Writing over it does not close the gap; it moves the gap to whoever approves the work.

04 · Time budget first

The time budget goes first.

The time budget goes above the direction. A reader who meets the solution first has formed an opinion before the cost arrives. Put the number early, say where it came from, and every later paragraph is read against whether the work is worth that time. The time budget is the size of the commitment the business is choosing.

05 · Enough to decide

Enough of the direction, and no more.

The direction appears as its parts, each one something a person can experience, with one honest line about what it gives up. Layout, copy, states, and component choices stay out. A builder weeks later needs room for the many decisions this document does not hold. Settling them here turns the proposal into a specification no one agreed to.

06 · Named with their reasons

Traps and cuts, each with a reason.

A rabbit hole is a specific place the work could vanish into, written with the decision that keeps it out. Naming the trap and pairing it with "not that part, this cycle" is what makes it a boundary. Exclusions work the same way, each with its reason and scoped to this cycle. An exclusion written as a permanent verdict gets reversed later with no record of why.

Surviving enterprise review. A proposal in enterprise B2B touches roles beyond the team, names what becomes auditable, depends on named systems of record, and states who outside the team has to agree before it ships. Any of these arriving after the decision is how a bounded project quietly becomes a bigger one.
07 · The traceability check

A slop pass is a traceability check.

Before it is presented, the draft runs through a slop pass that works as a traceability check. "Significantly faster" and "marks a pivotal moment" trace back to neither document, and each one that survives makes the traceable sentences around it look invented too. Puffery is also where a proposal starts selling without anyone choosing to sell. When a line reads thin because a number is missing, the number comes from the frame, or the line moves to what is still open.

A rabbit hole and its patch, in full. Rabbit hole: bulk actions could pull the team into building a general undo stack for every operation in the app. Patch: this cycle covers single-step undo on the bulk action only; multi-step and per-operation undo are a separate proposal. The pair names the specific trap and the decision that bounds it, which is what turns a worry on the page into a boundary.
08 · One read, four answers

A finished proposal can be answered four ways.

A finished proposal can be answered four ways. It can be approved, approved with a smaller time budget, sent back to close one named open item, or declined. All four are outcomes a good proposal is built for. One read is enough to choose any of them.

The payoff

A proposal is assembly, not authoring.

Nothing in a proposal is new. It carries the frame and the direction forward instead of rewriting them, refuses to be written while a required input is missing, puts the time budget above the direction, gives enough of the direction to decide and no more, and names rabbit holes and exclusions as decisions with reasons. Every sentence passes through a traceability check. A finished one can be answered four ways in a single read.
