A product design skill · How problem-framing works

Frame before you design.

A solution can look right and still solve the wrong problem. The further a project advances, the harder that becomes to admit. problem-framing pressure-tests the problem before design moves forward.

Scroll to pressure-test the frame

The job

Turn a fuzzy initiative into a clear, evidence-backed problem.

A product team often starts with a feature request, a complaint, or a solution already attached. problem-framing slows the commitment down just enough to separate what was observed from what was assumed—and to decide what problem is worth solving.

The skill helps the designer remember every important question that should be answered before proposing solutions. It asks them one at a time, follows the evidence, and keeps unresolved questions visible.

The output is a problem north star. The Framed Problem is a traceable account of the user, tension, outcome, evidence, assumptions, boundaries, and open question. It stays in the project so later sketches, scope choices, and decisions can be checked against the same intent.

Diagram 01 / 09Two entry points
01 · Entry

Two entry points.

The skill can enter at the beginning or in the middle of the work. A new project brings a raw idea; an active project brings decisions, design explorations, feedback, and accumulated cost. Both need a problem frame grounded in evidence.

What is being produced? Not a requirements document and not a proposed interface. A Framed Problem is the evidence-backed boundary that Shape can rely on.
02 · What do we know?

Read before asking.

Before asking the designer anything, the skill reads the available project record. Its first internal question is: What do we know, and what are we merely explaining or assuming? Facts found in the documents are not asked again.

03 · Question 1

What actually happened?

The request says, “Add an AI payroll-readiness dashboard.” The skill asks: Tell me about the most recent payroll run that was delayed. What was the HR admin trying to complete, what was missing, what did they do next, and what was the consequence? One concrete episode replaces a feature request.

04 · Question 2

Why do we think that is the cause?

The skill offers a working hypothesis, then asks: What makes us think a dashboard would prevent the delay? In five workflow interviews, HR admins understood the payroll summary, but four still delayed approval while chasing missing employee changes from managers. The causal map changes before the next question is chosen.

05 · Question 3

What changed since kickoff?

For work already in progress, the skill asks: What have we learned since the original brief, and which decisions does that learning reopen? The AI dashboard concept still counts as work completed, but the time spent creating it is not evidence that visibility was the real problem.

The question trail: What happened? Why do we think that is the cause? What changed? Which explanation fits? What evidence would change our choice? Which slice belongs in this project?
06 · Compare

Compare the possible problems.

The same payroll delay can support several problem statements, and each one leads to a different design space. The skill explains the alternatives, recommends the one best supported by observations, then asks: Which explanation fits what we saw—and what evidence would make us choose another? This prevents the first story from becoming fact by default.

07 · Question 4

What evidence would change the choice?

The skill proposes the exact input: In metrics, where do SMB payroll runs stall, and which missing-change types appear beforehand? In research, what episodes mention chasing managers, unconfirmed changes, or late payroll approval? It also states what result would support or weaken the frame.

08 · Question 5

Which slice belongs in this project?

The team may not need—or be able—to solve the whole problem. The skill asks: Which meaningful part can this project influence now, and what must stay outside? It defines a chosen slice without prescribing how to solve it.

One boundary, in concrete terms:
User
An SMB HR admin responsible for payroll.
Context
The 48 hours before payroll approval.
Desired outcome
The admin resolves missing changes and approves payroll on time.
Constraint
Managers remain responsible for submitting employee changes.
Time budget
Two weeks of build time. The solution must change or shrink to fit the time—not the other way around.
Major risk
The platform may not distinguish a missing change from “no change,” causing false alerts and eroding HR trust.
Optional boundary
Supported payroll configurations are not yet defined. If this stays open, Shape may spread across every configuration and exceed the two-week build budget.
Chosen slice
Surface and resolve missing changes before payroll approval—not redesign payroll or every manager workflow.
09 · Confirm

Is the problem ready for Shape?

Shape is the next phase: explore distinct solution hypotheses as rough Excalidraw sketches, evaluate their trade-offs, and pick one bounded direction—without producing a hi-fi design. The skill first checks that the critical problem questions were answered or explicitly left open. The confirmed Framed Problem becomes the project’s problem north star for Shape and all later work.

The decision after framing

Every status has a next move.

Problem Framing does not automatically send every initiative to Shape. Its status tells the designer exactly what should happen next.

Ready for Shape

Move forward. Use the Framed Problem as the north star while exploring rough approaches.

Revisit Shape

Re-evaluate the current approach. The problem frame changed; preserve valid decisions and reopen the ones the new evidence affects.

Continue current frame

Keep the project moving. New evidence still supports the user, tension, outcome, and boundary; continue while monitoring known risks.

Evidence needed

Ask a targeted question. Retrieve the exact metric or research input that can change the frame, then run framing again.

Needs reframing

Return to the problem questions. The user, cause, outcome, or chosen slice is not coherent enough to guide approaches.

Split

Create separate Framed Problems. The initiative contains independent users, tensions, or outcomes that should not share one direction.

Stop

Do not continue downstream work. Record why the problem lacks sufficient evidence, value, ownership, or relevance.

A concrete output

What a Framed Problem looks like.

The questions from the previous scenes consolidate into one concise project document. This is the problem north star—not a solution specification.

Project north starFRAMED_PROBLEM.md

SMB HR admins discover missing employee changes too late

Status
Ready for Shape
Source request
“Add an AI payroll-readiness dashboard.” The dashboard remains a proposed solution, not part of the problem definition.
Baseline story
An SMB HR admin tried to approve payroll, but several employee changes were missing or unconfirmed. They stopped to chase managers in chat and spreadsheets, delaying payroll approval.
Chosen problem frame
SMB HR admins discover missing employee changes only during final payroll review, forcing manual follow-up with managers and putting on-time approval at risk.
User and desired outcome
User: SMB HR admins responsible for payroll. Outcome: they identify and resolve missing changes early enough to approve payroll on time.
Evidence
In five payroll-workflow interviews, HR admins understood the payroll summary; four still delayed approval while chasing missing employee changes from managers.
Working question
How might we help SMB HR admins identify and resolve missing employee changes before payroll approval, without taking ownership away from managers?
Boundaries
  • In: readiness of employee-change inputs during the 48 hours before payroll approval.
  • Out: redesigning payroll calculation, every manager workflow, or the full HCM platform.
  • Constraint: managers remain responsible for submitting employee changes.
  • Time budget: two weeks of build time. The chosen solution must fit this fixed investment.
  • Optional boundary: supported payroll configurations are not yet defined.
  • Consequence if deferred: Shape may expand across every configuration, exceed the two-week budget, and reopen the approach during Design. Resolve before the design package.
Major risk
The platform may not reliably distinguish a missing employee change from a legitimate “no change” before payroll review. If that happens, false alerts could erode HR trust and make the chosen direction unusable.
Shape handoff
Explore materially different rough Excalidraw sketches. Each must preserve manager ownership, address early identification and resolution, and respond to the working question; none should assume a dashboard is the answer.

The payoff

Now shape the solution direction.

Design does not begin by choosing an answer. It begins by remembering the questions that make an answer meaningful. In Shape, each solution hypothesis becomes a rough Excalidraw sketch showing its mechanism, essential elements, relationships, and key interactions. The team evaluates the alternatives and picks one bounded direction. FRAMED_PROBLEM.md remains the problem north star used to judge every sketch and later decision.

The whole picture

Define the direction. Then design.

Problem Framing, Shape, Evaluate and Pick, and Design Package are direction-setting phases. They help the team choose what to pursue and keep the project from drifting toward the wrong problem or an unbounded solution. Design is where detailed interaction work, validation, and implementation guidance begin.

Before Design: define the problem north star, explore alternatives, evaluate and pick one approach, then secure commitment through the design package. Optional constraints may remain open only when their consequence and decision deadline are explicit.
Direction settingThese phases prevent drift before detailed design begins.

Shape

Draw materially different approaches, expose their trade-offs, and adapt them to the fixed build-time budget.

Output: candidate approaches

Choose one approach

Evaluate alternatives against the north star, evidence, time budget, constraints, and risk consequences; then pick one.

Output: bounded direction
04 · Propose the bet

Design Package

Present the problem, appetite, chosen shape, rabbit holes, and no-gos.

Output: decision-ready design package

05 · Detail and validate

Design

Make the detailed interaction and product decisions inside the approved direction. Validate behavior and support implementation without losing the problem north star.

Output: detailed design + learning

Method provenance

Three ideas informed this skill.

problem-framing is a synthesis, not a new methodology presented in isolation. Each source contributes a distinct part of its behavior.

A More Beautiful Question

Warren Berger’s Why → What if → How progression supplies the movement from challenging the situation, through alternative explanations, to an evidence-producing next step.

Visit the book’s official site

Shape Up

Shape Up is a product-development method created at Basecamp. It calls the pre-build phase shaping: define a rough solution direction and a fixed build-time budget, then adapt the solution to that time while making boundaries, risk consequences, and exclusions explicit.

grill-with-docs

Matt Pocock’s interviewing pattern supplies one question at a time, a recommended answer, facts discovered from project context, and shared understanding that remains documented.
