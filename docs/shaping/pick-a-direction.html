Pick a direction.

shaping is a product design skill. It takes a framed problem and works out which solution direction to build, inside a fixed time budget — and it ends with one direction and the reasoning that picked it.

The skill

Shaping settles which solution to build.

A team agrees on the problem but holds two different solutions, and nothing written down says which one the solution has to satisfy.

shaping is the second stage of the chain, after problem-framing and before proposal. It carries the framed problem forward and turns disagreement into a decision that can be checked.

Where it sits: shaping runs between knowing which problem to solve and knowing what to build, and it ends with one solution direction and the reasoning that picked it, not with wireframes.

Diagram 01 / 10Two-answer standoff
01 · The tension

Two answers, no test.

A team agrees on the problem. One person wants a guided wizard, another wants a batch endpoint. Both are real answers to it, and nothing written down says which one the solution has to satisfy.

02 · Carries the frame

In and out.

Shaping starts from FRAMED_PROBLEM.md and carries it forward word for word. It does not re-open the problem, the user, or the outcome. If the work turns up evidence that the frame is wrong, that goes back to problem-framing. What comes out is one file, DIRECTIONS.md.

03 · Requirements

A numbered list.

The first move is writing down what any solution has to be true of. Each one is a capability or a constraint, never a mechanism, and it gets a number. Each also gets a status, from Core goal down to Out, and that status is the scope dial. Nine at the top level is the ceiling.

Requirements read as tests, not features. "Users can see blockers" is a feature. "A payroll admin can tell, before the run starts, whether it will fail" is a test a direction either passes or fails.
04 · Enterprise classes

The list extends.

A framed problem rarely mentions who is allowed to act, what has to be logged, or how a direction behaves at forty thousand rows. Shaping walks a fixed list of these before any direction is drawn. Each one becomes a requirement or a recorded non-requirement. These are the constraints that invalidate a direction late, after everyone has agreed.

05 · Two directions

Different mechanisms.

Now come the directions, and there have to be at least two that differ in mechanism. Fixing the run at the moment it breaks is one mechanism. Moving the work earlier so the run opens clean is another. A rearrangement of the same idea is not a second direction.

From paragraph to mechanism. Each direction is made concrete with a breadboard — places, affordances, and connection lines, written in text — so the fit check runs against a mechanism instead of a paragraph. That is the breadboarding skill.
06 · Parts, not layers

Vertical against horizontal.

Each direction breaks into parts, and how you split it decides whether it can be cut later. UI, API, and database are layers, and nothing works until all three ship. Split into things a person can do, like detect a blocker or gate the close, and one part can be dropped while the rest still stand.

07 · Flag the unknown

One part lights amber.

Every part gets one test. Can the team describe how it works, not just what it does? A part that fails gets a flag, and a flag counts as a failure in the check that follows. It marks missing work, so it cannot be accepted the way a risk can.

08 · The fit check

Binary matrix.

The fit check scores every direction against every requirement. Each cell is a pass or a fail, and there is no third value. That constraint is the point, because a three-way scale lets every direction look respectable and settles nothing. When a direction passes everything and still feels wrong, a requirement is missing, so name it and run the check again.

Version lineage. A failed check is a brief, not a verdict. Fixing the failing part produces A2, then A3; a new letter is kept for a genuinely different mechanism. Superseded versions stay in the document, struck through with the reason, and only the current version appears in the matrix.
09 · Time and scope

Fixed bar, moving scope.

The time budget comes from the frame. It is the spend the business committed to, set before anyone estimated the work. When the chosen direction does not fit, scope moves and the deadline holds. Each cut becomes a new version, so the reason it was made survives.

Spikes. A flagged unknown on a leading direction gets one bounded investigation, with acceptance criteria phrased as "we can describe how…" rather than "we can decide if…". The spike buys understanding; the decision happens afterwards, back in the fit check.
10 · The exit

Five statuses.

Shaping ends by selecting one direction at one version, and by naming the requirement that separated it from the runner-up. When an unknown still blocks the leading direction, the honest exit is spike-needed, not a selection. When every candidate fails a Must-have, that result is worth more than a forced pick. The file that records all of it is DIRECTIONS.md.

The handoff. Shaping passes DIRECTIONS.md and FRAMED_PROBLEM.md to proposal, and leaves layout, copy, component choices, and edge cases open for Design to decide.

The payoff

One direction, at one version, with an honest status.

You can describe the method now. Requirements are written as capabilities and numbered, directions are scored against them in a binary fit check, unknowns are flagged instead of assumed, and the time budget stays fixed while scope moves. It ends with one direction at one version and an honest status.
