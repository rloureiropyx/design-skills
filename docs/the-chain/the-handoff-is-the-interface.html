A product design library · How the five skills work as one

The handoff is the interface.

Five skills that look independent in an index and are not. They form a chain, and the only thing that crosses from one link to the next is a document. That constraint is the method — everything else follows from it.

Scroll to follow the chain

The part an index cannot show

Five explainers, and none of them explains the system.

Each skill in this library has its own article. Read all five and you still will not have the thing that matters most: they are not five tools you pick between. They are one chain, and a chain is defined by its joints, not by its links.

This piece is about the joints. What crosses between two skills, what each one is allowed to change, what happens when a link is skipped, and where evidence goes when it contradicts a decision that was already made.

The rule the whole library rests on. A downstream skill carries what an upstream one decided. It never re-derives it. When it finds evidence that the earlier decision was wrong, it sends that back — it does not quietly restate the problem in new words and keep going.

01 · The tension

One project. Three problems.

A brief, a spec, and a deck, all about the same project. Each one states the problem in its own words, and no two are identical. Nobody wrote the wrong thing — three competent people each restated the problem, and now the project has three of them.

02 · Three roots

Each root has one permission.

Process skills change what the team believes. Artifact skills assemble the thing a group can act on. Renderers make a decision visible. The permissions are not a taxonomy — a renderer that is allowed to decide is how a diagram becomes an argument nobody agreed to.

03 · The chain

The arrows are the method.

The skills are not the interesting part of this picture. The arrows are. The only thing that crosses from one skill to the next is a document, and that document is the entire interface — no shared memory, no “as we discussed”, no context that lives only in a thread.

Why a document, and not a conversation? Because a conversation has no version, no citation, and no reader six weeks later. In enterprise work the person who approves the spend was not in the room where it was decided, the person who inherits the project joins in month three, and the auditor arrives after everyone has moved on. A document survives all three. A shared understanding survives none of them.
04 · Carry, never re-derive

A paraphrase forks the problem.

A downstream skill carries what the previous one decided. It quotes the line and cites where it came from. The moment it paraphrases instead, the problem has forked: two versions now exist, both defensible, and only one of them gets cited in the room.

05 · The gates

Every skill can refuse its input.

Framing buys time to shape, not permission to build. Shaping will not select a shape while an unknown is still flagged. The package will not be written over a missing appetite — and a skill that cannot refuse its input is a formatting tool, not a method.

06 · Skip a link

The cost is never paid where it was skipped.

Skip the frame and you get a shape that fits requirements nobody agreed to. Skip shaping and the appetite gets invented in the meeting, by whoever is most confident. Skip the package and the decision belongs to whoever spoke last.

Joining a project already in progress. The chain does not assume a clean start, and most real work is not one. An in-progress project already has these documents — implicitly, in someone’s head, in a thread, in a slide. Writing them down is not bureaucracy applied late; it is the step that surfaces the disagreement everyone has been routing around.
07 · Backflow

Contradicting evidence goes back.

Downstream work is where a frame gets tested, so downstream is where it breaks. Evidence that contradicts the frame goes back to the frame and reopens it. Fixing it locally — a new sentence in the shaping doc — is the exact failure the chain exists to catch, because now the two documents disagree and neither one says so.

08 · Renderers last

A tidy picture of confusion.

A renderer owns coordinates, contrast and legibility. It does not own the content. Hand one an undecided idea and it returns a clean diagram of that confusion — and a clean diagram gets believed, which is worse than the confusion was.

09 · The payoff

Enter anywhere. Skip nothing.

You can enter this chain anywhere. Start at shaping when the problem is genuinely settled; start at the package when the shape was chosen last quarter. What you cannot do is skip a document, because the next skill asks for it and stops. The handoff is the interface — that is the whole method.

What carrying looks like

The same sentence, three times, unchanged.

This is the entire discipline in one example. One line is written once, in the frame, with its evidence. It then appears in the shaping document and in the design package as a quotation with a source — not as an improved version of itself.

It looks almost pedantic on the page. It is the difference between a project with one problem and a project with three.

The problem
Payroll admins find blocking errors during the run, when there is no time left to resolve them without delaying pay.
Evidence
Observed in 12 of 14 admin interviews; 31% of runs in Q3 required at least one manual hold.

What a fork would look like. “Admins waste time on payroll errors” is a reasonable rewrite of that sentence. It is also a different problem: it drops when the errors are found, which is the only reason the timing-based shape was selected over the better error list. The paraphrase reads more cleanly and quietly deletes the requirement.

The payoff

One chain, and no rework.

Nothing in this library is decided twice. That is not a productivity claim — it is what makes a decision traceable six weeks later, defensible in a review nobody in the original room attends, and safe to reopen deliberately rather than by accident. The documents are the project’s memory. The skills are just what writes them.

Start where the uncertainty actually is. Arrive carrying FRAMED_PROBLEM.md, SHAPING.md or DESIGN_PACKAGE.md — or write the one you are missing before you go on.
