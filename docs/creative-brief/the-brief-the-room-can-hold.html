One selection, two records

A meeting brief is not a research repository.

Jared M. Spool described a client team that began design meetings by reading a short creative brief aloud. The brief aligned the room on the objective, the people, the situations, and the principles before anyone argued about the design.

creative-brief keeps that ritual and adds an evidence companion. The short file is what the room reads. The companion is what an agent reads before it changes the short file.

01 - Different briefs

The project name is shared. The design intent is not.

The meeting is called "off-cycle payroll correction," but the name is the only shared part. One person is designing for speed, another for approval, and another for audit. The team can discuss the same project while making different designs.

02 - Two files

One file for the room. One for the record.

The skill creates two files because one document cannot stay short and hold the whole record. CREATIVE_BRIEF.md is read aloud in about two minutes. Its evidence companion carries the sources, assumptions, candidate sets, and history an agent needs.

03 - Four fields

The caps force a selection.

The short brief has four fields and no fifth one. The objective centers the business problem and outcome. One or two research-backed personas, up to three user needs, and one or two principles select what matters now.

04 - Ask, then inspect

Start with the operator's view, then test it.

The skill always asks what is happening, why the business cares, who is affected, and what needs clarification. It then reads the project record for existing personas, observed user needs, principles, and constraints. Traceable sources get IDs and locators; the operator's account keeps its stated basis.

Enterprise detail stays available without becoming a fifth field

Roles, permissions, policy, auditability, integrations, data volume, operational failures, and accessibility can all change the selected persona, scenario, or principle. Their full detail stays in CREATIVE_BRIEF_EVIDENCE.md.

05 - Draft first

Give the operator a selection to challenge.

The skill writes a provisional draft instead of returning a template. That draft makes the research request specific. After previous research returns, the skill prefers existing personas, explains any necessary proposed variant, and asks one remaining decision at a time.

06 - Evidence states

Confidence belongs behind the sentence.

A short sentence can hide a weak basis. The companion keeps supported findings separate from assumptions, unknowns, commitments, and preferences. A persona does not become researched because the brief describes it confidently.

07 - One required prompt

Every pass retrieves previous research.

One Product Research Analyst prompt asks for evidence about the business problem, existing persona fit and shortcomings, observed user needs, and recurring trade-offs. Every answer needs an internal source and locator; No evidence found remains a valid result.

08 - Human handoff

The human carries the prompt, and the skill waits.

The skill never calls the analyst. The operator copies the prompt, pastes the cited response back, and the skill waits in between. No evidence found is a useful answer; an unsourced summary remains an assumption.

09 - Selective revision

New evidence reopens one field, not the interview.

Returned research does not restart the interview. Each finding maps to the question it answers, and only the affected brief field reopens. The previous assumption stays in the change history instead of disappearing.

The file read at the meeting

Project Objective

Retroactive employee changes create correction risk after payroll close. The business needs corrections completed before submission without losing confidence in the result.

Key Persona

A multi-state payroll specialist responsible for final validation and audit response.

Key Scenarios

  1. A payroll specialist needs to understand what changed after a retroactive termination so they can submit a defensible correction.
  2. An approver needs to trace why a recalculation changed so they can approve it confidently.

Key Principle

Audit trail over invisible automation. When speed and explainability conflict, preserve a visible account of what changed and why.

10 - Read and review

Agreement makes it ready for the meeting.

The team reads the completed brief and asks whether it still describes what they are designing today. Agreement makes it ready for the meeting, not approval to build. At the next review, no change keeps the version; a confirmed field change updates both files together.

Independent by design

The skill owns one alignment artifact and its evidence.

creative-brief begins with the operator's current account, tests it against previous internal research, and ends with a confirmed short brief plus its evidence companion. It requires no upstream document and implies no downstream workflow.

It begins with

The business problem and outcome, the operator's current view, available project context, and a required previous-research prompt.

It ends with

Research-backed personas or explained variants, user needs, decision principles, and two synchronized files ready for the next design discussion.
