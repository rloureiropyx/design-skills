What it is

Sketch the solution in words first.

Breadboarding sketches a solution in words instead of pixels, at a fidelity too coarse to express layout, so the mechanism is arguable before the design is.

It names three things and stops. That is the whole notation, and it cannot express layout — which is exactly why you can argue about the mechanism without arguing about the visual design at the same time.

Places are anywhere a person can be. Affordances are what a person can see or do in a place. Connection lines say which action leads where.

01 · The sentence

A solution sentence, alone.

A team lands on one line for its solution. "A place where a payroll admin can see unapproved timesheets and chase them before the run locks." It sounds finished. Ask two people to picture it and they picture different screens, and neither can start building.

02 · The notation

Three symbols, and nothing else.

A breadboard names three things and stops. Places are anywhere a person can be. Affordances are what a person can see or do in a place, and connection lines say which action leads where. It is too coarse to show layout, and that is the point.

Why it is worth ten minutes. A sentence like the one in scene 1 hides four questions — where the person starts, what they see, what they can do, and where each of those leads. Finding the one with no answer costs ten minutes here and a week after a wireframe. Note that breadboarding carries the framed problem from problem-framing rather than re-deriving it; the frame here is that payroll admins cannot tell, before the run locks, which timesheets are still unapproved.
03 · The starting place

The first box.

Start with the place the person is actually in when the problem happens, not a guess. In this example that is the payroll run checklist, where the admin works the day before lock. A solution that begins somewhere nobody visits is a solution nobody uses. This is the most skipped step and the most often wrong.

04 · What they see and do

Affordances fill the place.

In every place, write what the person can see and what they can do. On the checklist the admin sees the runs due and a count of timesheets still unapproved. They can open the list of who is missing, and mark the run ready. A place with only actions has nothing to act on, and a place with only information is a dead end.

05 · The lines

Connections drawn, one dangling.

Every action has to lead somewhere. Open the list of who is missing leads to the unapproved timesheets, a second place. Chase an approver has no place to lead to yet, so its line ends in the air. That dangling line is the most useful thing the breadboard has found so far.

What a place is and is not. A place is somewhere a person can be sent to, not a section of a screen or a state of a component. An empty list, a loading list, and a failed list are the same place showing different things, written as [see] affordances rather than promoted to new places.
06 · Walk the wrong paths

The unhappy paths light up.

Read it as the user, then walk the paths where things go wrong. When nothing is unapproved, the checklist needs something to show other than an empty box. When a manager without unlock rights lands here, half the actions are not theirs. And once the admin opens a timesheet, there is no drawn way back to the checklist.

07 · The least-privileged role

The place, redrawn for a manager.

Redraw the starting place as the least privileged person who reaches it. A line manager can see the unapproved timesheets for their team but cannot mark the run ready. That version of the place has fewer affordances, and the gap is a finding, not a detail to smooth over. Delegation raises the same question, since someone chasing on another admin's behalf may need a place of their own.

08 · Flag, don't fill

The gap marked.

Mark the chase-an-approver action with a warning rather than inventing where it goes. The line might need a reminder screen, or it might do nothing a person can observe, or it might be a step nobody has decided. Naming which of the three keeps an open question open. Filled in with something plausible, it would read a week later as a decision the team never made.

The rest of the enterprise pass. Approval and hand-off, audit (where a person later reads what happened, which is itself a place), bulk and volume, what the place shows when the upstream system is stale, and the keyboard path including where focus lands after each connection line.
09 · Your first move

The finished breadboard.

The breadboard found three things the sentence hid. There is no way back from the timesheet, a line manager cannot finish the task, and chasing an approver leads nowhere yet. Take one solution sentence you are holding and write down the place the person starts. Then list what they can see and do there, and follow the first action that has nowhere to go.

Where breadboarding sits in the chain. After problem-framing, before shaping, feeding a wireframe only once layout turns out to be the real question. A flag here becomes a ⚠ in DIRECTIONS.md and an automatic failure in shaping's fit check. The text breadboard is the artifact; a drawing is a copy of it, handed to tldraw-skill only after the words are settled.

What a breadboard finds

Four holes a sentence hides.

Each one is cheap to find here and expensive to find after a wireframe. The names come from walking the breadboard as the user, not from the happy path.

The starting place

Most commonly skipped, most commonly wrong. A solution that begins somewhere nobody goes is a solution nobody uses.

Actions with no destination

The place does not exist, the action does nothing observable, or a step is undecided. All three are worth saying out loud.

Paths with no way back

A breadboard reads fine forwards and frequently has no return path. The missing return is a coral line, not a detail.

Gaps

Anything the team can name but not describe gets a warning flag. An absence, not a risk — never filled with something plausible.

The payoff

Now breadboard your own sentence.

You can breadboard a solution sentence yourself. You know the three-element notation, you have watched it surface a missing return path, a role that cannot finish, and an action with no destination, and you leave with a first move you can make on your own sentence.
