Work out the problem before you design the solution.

Fifteen skills for product design work: start with an evidence-backed problem frame or a short creative brief, try two or three directions and pick one, then write it up so people can decide. The rest draw, diagram, explain or publish the result.

Process

Skills that end in a decision rather than a deliverable.

  • Process · 01

    Frame before you design

    problem-framing

    Turns a request into a written problem with the evidence it rests on: quotes kept apart from interpretations, more than one explanation considered, and what was set aside written down. Has a quick mode — five questions, one page — for small solo work.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Buys time to explore solutions. Does not buy permission to build.

  • Process · 02

    The brief the room can hold

    creative-brief

    Creates a Project Objective, one or two Key Personas, up to three Key Scenarios, and one or two Key Principles: a short brief the team can read aloud before design discussion. A separate evidence companion holds the sources, assumptions, longer context, and one consolidated Product Research Analyst prompt when previous research could change the selection.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    An alternative start beside problem framing, not another document in the main chain.

  • Process · 03

    Pick a direction

    shaping

    Two people can hold two defensible solutions with no shared test between them. This writes the test down: numbered requirements, two or three lettered directions, a fit check with only two possible answers, and unknowns flagged so they cannot be waved through. Directions improve across versions, and discarded ones stay visible with the reason.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Stops at one selected direction with no unresolved ⚠️ flags.

  • Process · 04

    Context intake

    context-intake

    A transcript pasted into a repo is not context — it is the same raw material, now diluting every retrieval made against it. This splits what was said from what is true, files each part in the container that owns it, and keeps the map current. One question routes everything: is this true because of this project, or regardless of it?

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    Never commits the raw source, and never writes to design/.

Artifacts

Skills that produce the thing people react to.

  • Artifacts · 01

    Breadboard before you draw it

    breadboarding

    Sketches a solution in words instead of pixels: the places a person can be, what they can do or see in each one, and which action takes them where. Too coarse to express layout, which is what lets people argue about the mechanism first. Shaping uses it to make each direction concrete.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Nine scenes on arguing about the mechanism before anyone opens a canvas.

  • Artifacts · 02

    The drawing is the last decision

    wireframes

    Decides what a screen must do, what goes on it, and how priority reads, then hands that to a renderer. Scale, fidelity, annotation weight and abstraction are four separate axes, so "more detail" adds content, states and rules before it adds polish. Colour means something or it is not there, and one screen shows one real moment.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Nine scenes on the four axes that "make it higher fidelity" hides.

  • Artifacts · 03

    The drawing is the easy part

    diagrams

    Decides what a diagram should show and why before anything is drawn: intention, audience, scope, scale. The type is chosen from what the reader is stuck on — time, arrangement or context — not from the word they used. Twelve recipes, a stopping rule, and a key that is never allowed to become a decoder ring.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    A diagram fails before anything is drawn — nine scenes on the four decisions that come first.

  • Artifacts · 04

    One read, one decision

    proposal

    The document people read once and then decide on: the problem, the time budget, the chosen direction, the rabbit holes, what is deliberately out, and what is still uncertain. Everything carried from the two documents before it, and it refuses to be written while an unknown is still flagged.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Decidable in one read, or it is not finished.

  • Artifacts · 05

    Pitch

    pitch

    What you are going to say when you explain a decision to people who were not in the room, and in what order. Audience, goal, tone, a spine, and beats — each one a claim written as a complete sentence, the evidence behind it, what must be visible for it to land, and what breaks if it is cut. It renders nothing, so the same narrative can become an article, a deck, or both.

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    A claim is a sentence. “Results” is a heading.

Renderers

Skills that materialise a decision made elsewhere. Give one an undecided idea and it will produce a tidy picture of confusion.

  • Renderers · 01

    How a scrollytelling article gets made

    scrollytelling

    Long-form web articles whose diagrams change as you scroll. It reads the pitch and maps its beats onto scenes — merging two when the graphic does not change between them — then approval, then the build, and the result is checked across a device matrix rather than admired on a laptop.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    Every article on this page was produced by this skill.

  • Renderers · 02

    Slides

    slides

    The same pitch, as a deck. It splits where scrollytelling merges, because a fresh surface costs nothing and lets the presenter control when each piece lands. Every slide title is the beat’s claim as a complete sentence — read the titles alone and the deck should tell the whole story. Renders through Figma Slides.

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    External dependency. Without Figma it hands back an approved deck spec and stops.

  • Renderers · 03

    Draw what was decided

    tldraw-skill

    Draws flows, journey maps, service blueprints, concept models, sitemaps, breadboards and wireframes on a local canvas. It owns coordinates, connectors, contrast and legibility — never the content. Needs the tldraw offline app running on the same machine.

    • Explainer
    • Scene script
    • SKILL.md

    External runtime dependency — read the notes before installing.

Utilities

Skills the others call while they draft. They own neither a decision nor an artifact, and they hold the profiles the rest of the library reads.

  • Utilities · 01

    Writing Style

    writing-style

    Turns evidence of how you write into a profile every drafting skill loads first: voice in a paragraph, a few signature moves with the line quoted underneath each, defaults you can check a draft against, and the tics an editing pass must leave alone. It records what you do and what you are going for separately, and when they disagree you decide once instead of getting the average. Three ways in: samples you already have, a two-prompt exercise, or a voice note if you would rather talk than type.

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    For explaining and proposing. Product copy comes from the design system.

  • Utilities · 02

    Visual Style

    visual-style

    What writing-style does for how things read, for what they look like. A palette with named roles, a type pair and scale, density, radius, stroke weight, illustration idiom — tokens a renderer can consume rather than adjectives it has to interpret. Three presets on one axis, what carries the meaning: Paper for prose, Whiteboard for the sketch, Blueprint for the data. Pick one and move.

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    Accessibility floors are not stylable. The contract out-ranks every token.

  • Utilities · 03

    Slop Check

    slop-check

    Removes seventeen named patterns — throat-clearing openers, colon reveals, importance puffery, fake-profound endings — and nothing else. Asked what is wrong instead of asked for a fix, it names each pattern and quotes the line. It will not score writing and will not guess whether AI wrote it: detectors guess, a quoted line is evidence.

    • Explainer
    • SKILL.md

    A tic your profile protects is not a finding. That precedence is the whole point.
